    ZORA

    A conceptual technology accessory brand modelled after the aesthetic of BURGA and tailored for a digital native generation.

    The project is 17 files of vanilla HTML, CSS and JavaScript. Its focus is interaction refinement and human computer interaction principles, not commercial use. Every element follows a 25 point framework covering cognitive demand, visual perception and error management.

    Interaction decisions
    • People browse by mood, such as dark and moody, clean and minimal, or color pop, instead of category.
    • A mega menu based on Miller’s theory of information processing limits keeps navigation easy to scan.
    • Checkout drafts save automatically, and cart updates include an undo notification.
    • Product pages offer high quality zoom, and a cinematic loading screen builds the logo on first visit.
    Scope

    A layout prototype with a small product selection and free stock photography. It runs through client side simulation, with no backend, payments or inventory.

    VAULTÉ

    A high fidelity luxury e-commerce prototype built for my Human Computer Interaction course, deployed on Vercel.

    The aim was to turn the usability failures found in existing platforms, such as weak system feedback and high cognitive load, into working patterns guided by Nielsen’s heuristics and WCAG. Figma was used for wireframes and mockups, and the build uses React 18, TypeScript and Tailwind CSS.

    HCI decisions and where they appear
    • Visibility of system status: skeleton cards hold the layout while results load, and cart and wishlist badges update instantly.
    • User control: removing an item opens a 5 second undo window, and a broken link leads to a branded 404 with a countdown home.
    • Error prevention: Add to Cart stays disabled until size and colour are chosen, and checkout fields show live red or green borders.
    • Efficiency: Ctrl+K opens universal search for expert users, and filters wait 600 ms to avoid flicker.
    • Information architecture: top level navigation is limited to three segments, following Hick’s law.
    • Accessibility: gold focus rings, semantic HTML, ARIA roles on the live chat, and touch targets of 44 px or more.

    A UI/UX analysis of BestSecret

    A web audit of the members only fashion platform bestsecret.com, scored against Nielsen’s 10 usability heuristics.

    7.2/10overall score
    10heuristics rated

    Each heuristic is rated with annotated screenshots, with strengths and recommendations marked separately. Two findings shaped my later work: filtering turns the page entirely white with no loading state, and removing a wishlist item offers no undo.

    The fixes I recommended, a skeleton loading state and a 5 second undo toast, are the same patterns I built into VAULTÉ.
